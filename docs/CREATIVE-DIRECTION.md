# The Toybox creative bar

Toybox gives ordinary objects impossible jobs. Its distinct point of view lives in editable staging: a mug can commute, a doorway can look for a home, and a tiny tree can be the guest of honor. Every new feature should help someone discover, direct, or share a visual idea.

## What earns a place

1. **One clear impossibility.** Describe the idea in a sentence without relying on “cinematic,” “epic,” or a fashionable visual style. A different background alone is not a new story.
2. **Visible consequences.** Put the idea into the actual cast, scale, positions, timing, or camera. A title and a longer prompt are not enough.
3. **A turn in the story.** Establish an expectation, change it, and land on a legible final image. Three scenes must do three different jobs.
4. **A reason to care.** Surprise can be funny, tender, strange, or uneasy. Every cast member should support that intention.
5. **Something the maker can change.** Expose the staging and the rule. Keep props, poses, and direction editable; preserve the original when offering an experiment.
6. **Honest evidence.** Label stage plans, concept artwork, and generated footage accurately. Do not present a prompt as proof that a model can perform an action.

Reject starters that repeat the same chase in another location, random adjective generators, filters presented as stories, and novelty controls that do not change the resulting work.

## Three authored story sparks

| Film | Impossible job | Story progression | Creative intention |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The city crosses first | Buildings become commuters; the car waits. | The street wakes up → the buildings cross → a little one is late. | Deadpan comedy through reversed expectations and a delayed punchline. |
| A door looking for a home | A doorway searches for somewhere to belong. | No address → the wrong room → home has no walls. | A quiet search that ends by framing a lonely tree. |
| A parade for the last tree | A moon outpost celebrates its smallest inhabitant. | Someone arrives → a space is made → nobody leaves alone. | Tenderness conveyed by gathering, making room, and moving together. |

Each starter creates a separate project with three three-second scenes. Cast placement continues across cuts; camera changes have a purpose. The gallery shows real top-down stage plans from the project data. Starting a film saves the earlier project first.

## Director’s dares

These are concrete transformations of a scene, each appended as a separate take that can be undone:

- **Let the scenery lead:** hold the first prop still while the surrounding set moves, viewed through a locked wide camera.
- **Make the little thing enormous:** enlarge the first prop, shrink its surroundings, and lower the camera to make the scale reversal visible.
- **Close the loop:** return every prop to its opening pose; keep or introduce a detour in the middle.

The first prop is explicitly named as the lead. Uploaded photos remain in the original; the new take uses the editable miniature stage. The loop is exact in stage poses, not a promise of seamless generated video.

## Generation and creative judgment

The bundled local LTX workflow receives all three keyframes as guide frames, plus direction. Guides steer generated motion strongly but not exactly; the model still fills the movement between them. Render completion proves the pipeline works, not that a story's visual joke or emotional beat succeeded. Makers should watch the result, adjust staging and direction, select takes, and trim their cut.

The technical tests protect fresh project identity, independent copies, preserved photos, valid poses, inter-scene continuity, meaningful transformations, and portable project data. Human creative review still decides whether the premise reads, the ending lands, and a new addition deserves to be here.
